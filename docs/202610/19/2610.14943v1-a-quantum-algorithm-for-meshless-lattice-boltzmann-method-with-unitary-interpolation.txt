# A quantum algorithm for meshless lattice Boltzmann method with unitary interpolation 

Chi Zhang a, Hefeng Wang b, Hua Xiang a, ∗

> aSchool of Mathematics and Statistics, Wuhan University, Wuhan, China
> bSchool of Physics, Xi’an Jiaotong University, Xi’an, China

Abstract 

We present a mass-weighted meshless formulation for the shifted incompress-ible lattice Boltzmann method (LBM) whose streaming step is exactly uni-tary, combining with the Carleman embedding. In traditional meshless LBM, on a scattered node set the semi-Lagrangian streaming step is an interpo-lation, which is not unitary; a generic block encoding of this matrix there-fore requires a normalization proportional to its sparsity and logarithmically many ancillas. To overcome the non-unitarity, we instead express the inter-polation as the exponential exp( aD x + bD y ) of mimetic derivative operators. Endowing each node with its Voronoi mass and applying a mass-weighted similarity transform renders these derivative operators skew-Hermitian by enforcing moment constraints together with low-wavenumber trigonometric differentiation conditions, so that the interpolation becomes exactly unitary. Such unitarization of interpolation leave the local collision step unchanged up to a rescaling of its quadratic term, where the same weights define the amplitude encoding of the distribution functions. For quasi-uniform node sets, the mass-weighting diagonal remains within a unit-order normalization that degrades only mildly as the Carleman truncation order grows, thereby mitigating the cost of block encoding. Numerical experiments on the Taylor-Green vortex confirm that the method reproduces the full meshless LBM, with the velocity error decreasing as the Carleman order increases.

1. Introduction 

Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) remains one of the most demanding areas of scientific computing, and quantum computers have been proposed as a route to accelerating the solution of the partial differential equations underlying incompressible flow. The lattice Boltzmann method (LBM) is particularly attractive in this direction: it replaces the nonlinear Navier-Stokes system by a kinetic description in which a small set of distribution functions obey a linear streaming step and a local, algebraic collision step [1]. The locality of the collision operator makes the nonlinearity tractable, while the streaming step is a pure shift on the lattice. For a quantum setting, the collision nonlinearity can be addressed by Carleman linearization [2, 3], which embeds the finite-dimensional nonlinear dynamics into an infinite-dimensional linear system on the tensor powers of the state. Truncating this hierarchy at order NC yields a finite but high di-mensional linear evolution [4, 5, 6]. Applying the Carleman linearization to the lattice Boltzmann equation, the entire time step becomes a single linear map on the Carleman vector y = ( g, g⊗2, . . . , g⊗NC ), so that a collision-and-streaming update reduces to repeated application of a high-dimensional matrix. Several quantum lattice Boltzmann algorithms based on this con-struction have been proposed [7, 8, 9], and recent work has analyzed the Carleman truncation error and the block-encoding cost of the full incom-pressible flow solver, including nontrivial boundary conditions [10, 11]. Acentral conclusion of these analyses is that the quantum resources are gov-erned by the subnormalization factors of the block-encoded operators, which must be controlled for the algorithm to retain a useful complexity of the quantum linear system solver. Classically, the restriction of LBM to uniform Cartesian lattices is a se-rious practical limitation. To address this limitation, many methods have been developed to lift it by allowing the nodes to move. In the interpolation-supplemented LBM of He, Luo and Dembo the streaming step is performed on a non-uniform mesh by interpolating the populations back onto the ref-erence nodes [12]; the least-squares (meshless) LBM of Shu, Niu and Chew instead constructs Taylor-expansion based local interpolation and derivative operators on scattered nodes [13], and radial basis function (RBF) variants have since been developed [14, 15]. In all of these the streaming step is written as 

fm(X, t + ∆ t) = f Cm (X − em∆t, t ) ≈ Pm f Cm (X, t ),

2where X = {xi}Ni=1 is the scattered node set and Pm is a translation inter-polation matrix that transfers the m-th population from X to the departure set Gm = X − em∆t. The matrix Pm is sparse (each row contains only a few nonzero coefficients determined by a local stencil), but not unitary. This last fact is the obstruction that the present paper addresses. To em-bed the flow step into a quantum circuit one must block-encode the streaming matrix, and a generic sparse non-unitary matrix does not admit a cheap em-bedding: its block-encoding subnormalization αPm factor is of the order of the sparsity, and it requires Θ(log N) ancilla qubits [16, 17]. Under the Carleman hierarchy these factors compound, degrading the success probability roughly as α−NC 

> Pm

. We therefore seek a formulation in which the meshless translation is exactly unitary. The key observation is that a translation matrix can be obtained by evolving under a generator given by a linear combination of the derivative matrices Dx and Dy . If Dx and Dy are skew-Hermitian, then the streaming operator is unitary. Therefore, it can be embedded with normal-ization factor α = 1 and no ancilla, and can be time-stepped using standard quantum simulation machinery [18, 16]. The difficulty is that RBF or least-squares derivative matrices built on a scattered set are not skew-Hermitian by construction. The key to restoring unitarity is to give the scattered node set a mimetic structure: following structure-preserving (mimetic) discretizations, which re-produce continuous operator identities such as integration by parts at the discrete level [19, 20], each node carries the area of its Voronoi cell—the discrete counterpart of the diagonal Hodge star in discrete exterior calculus [21]—and these masses define a discrete mass-weighted inner product. Un-der the corresponding similarity transformation, a derivative matrix that is skew-symmetric in this weighted inner product becomes skew-symmetric in the ordinary Euclidean inner product, so that its matrix exponential is an exactly unitary translation. The very same Voronoi weights determine how the distribution functions are encoded as quantum amplitudes, and we show that this encoding leaves the local collision rule intact up to an inconsequen-tial rescaling of its quadratic term. For quasi-uniform node sets the Voronoi areas are mutually comparable, the mass-weighting diagonal then admits a block encoding with unit-order subnormalization and, crucially, a single an-cilla. We validate the resulting mass-weighted meshless quantum LBM on the Taylor-Green vortex against the full meshless LBM, for Carleman truncation orders 1, 2 and 3, and demonstrate the expected improvement in accuracy as the truncation order increases. 3The closest quantum LBM constructions to our setting are the interpolation-supplemented QLBM of [22] and its Taylor-series/least-squares fractional-step variant of [23]. Both, however, operate on a nonuniform Cartesian mesh rather than a genuinely scattered, meshless node set, and both replace the interpolation/derivative matrix by an SVD while assuming that the resulting dense unitary factors can be efficiently implemented, which is not justified for a general dense unitary matrix. Moreover, because a non-unitary collision operator must be reconstructed at every time step, the overall success prob-ability compounds multiplicatively and decays exponentially in the number of steps. Our mass-weighted formulation avoids these bottlenecks: streaming operator is a single, exactly unitary that is reused at every time step, leaving only the local collision step to be embedded. Besides, Carleman lineariza-tion and the resulting history state avoid the formation of a new non-unitary collision operator at each time step. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 recalls the shifted incompressible LBM on uniform Cartesian lattices and its Carleman embedding. In Section 3 we introduce the interpolation-supplemented mesh-less lattice Boltzmann method, expose the non-unitarity of its interpolation matrix, and explain the resulting difficulties for a quantum implementation. Section 4 develops the mimetic, mass-weighted derivative operators and the resulting unitary translation operator, together with the amplitude encoding of the distribution functions. The block-encoding cost is also analyzed. Nu-merical experiments on the Taylor-Green vortex are reported in Section 5, and conclusions are drawn in Section 6.

3.1. Interpolation-based meshless LBM 

The conventional LBM typically relies on a regular lattice to discretize the computational domain, which can limit its applicability to problems in-volving complex geometries. To overcome this limitation, meshless lattice Boltzmann methods have been developed, in which the computational do-main is represented by a set of scattered points rather than a regular lattice. In a meshless spatial discretization, where the computational points may be nonuniformly distributed or randomly scattered, the streaming step trans-ports the distribution function gm(r, t ) at r to the position r + em. However, the resulting position r + em generally does not coincide with any of the scattered points. Therefore, the conventional streaming operator needs to be modified. In contrast, the collision step is local in space and is therefore unaffected by the choice of spatial discretization. For a non-uniform scattered point set X = {xi}Ni=1 , we define a family of ghost point sets {Gm}Qm=1 by Gm = X − em. Here, the scattered points are expressed in lattice units, with the lattice spacing taken as the reference length scale and ∆x = ∆ t = 1 . In the meshless setting, ∆x does not rep-resent the spacing between neighboring computational points; instead, em

specifies the unit lattice displacement associated with the mth discrete ve-locity direction. An interpolation operator Pm : R|X| → R|Gm| is constructed to interpolate the distribution function gm(t), originally defined on X, onto the ghost set Gm. The streaming step can then be rewritten as

4.1. The Voronoi mass matrix as the canonical metric 

For non-uniform scattered points, the standard equally weighted inner product is generally inappropriate. Instead, a weighted inner product should be employed to account for the non-uniform distribution of the points. For an exponential exp( A) to be unitary, the generator A must be skew-Hermitian with respect to the inner product in which unitarity is measured. 4.2. Mimetic skew-Hermitian discretization of the generators. 

For each node xi, we define its stencil as the set of its q nearest neighbors under periodic boundary conditions and Euclidean distance, excluding the point itself. The collection of all such stencils induces a directed graph G =(X, E ), where a directed edge e = ( i, j ) ∈ E if and only if xj belongs to the stencil of xi. 6. Conclusion 

In this paper, we propose a quantum interpolation-supplemented mesh-less lattice Boltzmann method based on Carleman embedding. The probabil-ity density function was re-encoded through mass weighting, and a mimetic differential matrix was constructed as the generator of translation interpo-lation matrix, which is also the streaming operator. Its advantage is that the streaming step maintains the same unitary property as classical LBM. Meanwhile, we also demonstrate that the collision step changes caused by re-encoding have a minimal impact on the degradation of matrix block encoding complexity. Numerically, we validated the effectiveness of our proposed algo-rithm and its convergence with Carleman truncation order on Taylor Green problems with full periodic boundary conditions. Due to the requirement of unitary translation interpolation operator, our algorithm is suitable for full periodic boundary conditions, and further research on non-periodic boundary conditions problems will be conducted in the future.
