The first-principles quantum simulation of the full electronic Hamiltonian of a material model of realistic size appears daunting even in a future era of fully fault-tolerant quantum computers. In contrast, classical simulations of material excited states rely on heuristic quasiparticle frameworks, such as the GW approximation and Bethe-Salpeter equation, where only a few quasiparticles are explicitly considered. Here we give a detailed description of quasiparticle quantum simulation for multi-excitonic states within the BSE. We develop an explicit first-quantized, orbital-based block encoding of the multi-exciton BSE Hamiltonian for qubitization, and reduce its cost using inte-gral factorization and crystal symmetries. For a materials model of size N , and a fixed number of quasiparticles, the resulting algorithms require eO(N ) Toffoli gates and O(log N ) logical qubits, or, using space-time tradeoffs, eO(√N ) Toffoli gates and eO(√N ) logical qubits, achieving up to a sixth power reduction in Toffoli count, or up to exponential reduction in qubit count, versus standard full Hamiltonian simulation techniques that have been explored in materials simulation. Resource estimates for the classically challenging problem of triexcitonic eigenvalue estimation across a range of semiconductor models of a realistic physical size of O(10 3) atoms, show that our Toffoli-optimized implementation reduces the Toffoli count by 10 6 − 10 7 relative to full Hamiltonian simulation es-timates, while a qubit-optimized implementation requires only a few hundred logical qubits. More broadly, these results show that classical heuristic frameworks will be essential in the design of future fault-tolerant quantum algorithms for materials. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

The quantum simulation of materials is a highly an-ticipated application of quantum computing [1–12]. For many materials, the key questions involve the electronic structure, spectroscopy, and dynamics. These can be addressed by solving the time-independent and time-dependent electronic Schr¨ odinger equations. In the context of first principles fault-tolerant quantum materials simulation, computing the electronic Hamil-tonian eigenstates and dynamics has already been ex-plored [4, 9, 10, 13, 14]. These formulations have focused on exact simulations of the full electronic Hamiltonian of the material. However, since current first principles ma-terial simulations with density functional theory often involve a few thousand atoms and O(10 4) electrons or more, replacing such classical simulations is a formidable task. As we detail below, using existing techniques of full Hamiltonian simulation, the Toffoli counts needed to compute an excitation energy to a relevant precision ex-ceed even the resources that are expected to be available in the era of full fault-tolerance by many orders of magni-tude. Additional approximations must therefore be made for the quantum simulation of materials to be practical. Full Hamiltonian simulation is not the problem that is tackled by most beyond density functional classical

approaches. Instead, these work within a quasiparticle picture of electronic behaviour. This limits the explicit modeling of correlation effects to a few (quasi)particles. While the quasiparticle equations are augmented with additional heuristic assumptions that mean that they are not exact, their domain of applicability is understood through empirical validation. For example, the GW and GW/Bethe-Salpeter quasiparticle theories are probably the most commonly applied electronic structure simula-tions in materials beyond density functional theory [15– 18], and have found particular value in the modeling of semiconductor excitations [19, 20]. Recently, we have described the formulation of quan-tum simulation within the quasiparticle approximation and have shown that, in a setting that takes advantage of low-energy quasiparticles as well as locality, and un-der standard initial state preparation assumptions, quan-tum algorithms can achieve a large asymptotic speedup over classical algorithms within the same heuristic frame-work [21]. Here, we move beyond asymptotic scaling and provide a detailed analysis of the quasiparticle GW/BSE for excited state properties and dynamics. We focus only on the quasiparticle heuristic rather than also using lo-cality, as this more general setting covers a wider range of materials, and provides a baseline for the incorporation of more heuristic information in the future. We focus on the modeling of high order multi-excitonic states and dy-namics in semiconductors, which is extremely expensive and which has yet to be studied in first principles classi-cal simulations. We achieve, relative to full Hamiltonian arXiv:2610.02916v1 [quant-ph] 2 Oct 2026 2quantum simulation approaches that have been bench-marked in materials, a ∼ 6th order polynomial speedup in scaling when optimizing for Toffoli count, and up to an exponential compression when optimizing for qubit count. Relative to standard classical matrix-vector algo-rithms for the Bethe-Salpeter equation, the speedup is exponential in the multi-exciton order m, and for the tri-excitonic case, corresponds to a ∼ 12th order speedup in speed and an exponential reduction in classical resources. We perform explicit compilation of quasiparticle quan-tum simulation, working within a first-quantized, orbital basis formulation. Our compilation has some features in common with that of first- and second-quantized Hamil-tonian simulation compilation, such as the use of factor-ized integral representations, qubit/Toffoli data loading tradeoffs, and symmetry adaptation, but we use a new block encoding strategy that is adapted to the physi-cal form of the GW/BSE Hamiltonian. We carry out quantum resource estimates of tri-excitonic state simula-tion for a range of semiconductor materials, where we find ∼ 10 6 − 10 7 reduction in Toffoli-gate count rela-tive to full Hamiltonian simulation using realistic basis sets and problem sizes of a few thousand atoms. Our work therefore provides quantitative evidence of the ad-vantages of quasiparticle quantum simulation in future first-principles studies of materials using quantum com-puters.

> B. General nature of speedups versus classical and standard quantum simulation

We may now choose to solve the GW-BSE quasipar-ticle Schrodinger equations with standard quantum al-gorithms, for example quantum phase estimation for the eigenvalue problem and Hamiltonian simulation for the dynamics problems. The main difference from the stan-dard quantum simulation problem is that it now involves only mtot quasiparticles, counting electrons and holes to-gether. The Hilbert space is polynomial in size and thus the classical GW-BSE simulation methods are polyno-mial cost in N , which is why they are widely used. The quantum speedup we can expect is therefore a polyno-mial one. We can now establish the origin of the quantum versus classical speedup and the speedup of quasiparti-cle quantum algorithms versus standard full Hamiltonian simulation quantum algorithms. The classical linear algebra algorithm involves linear algebra in a Hilbert space of dimension N mtot . With-out additional heuristic structure, we store the classical vector with space N mtot , and the cost is that of oper-ating on it with the Hamiltonian operator. For a typi-cal 2-body Hamiltonian whose integral matrix elements can be written as a N 2 × N 2 matrix, the cost of apply-ing the Hamiltonian by a matrix-vector product has a 

O(N mtot +2 ) cost. In the quasiparticle quantum simulation, one is carry-ing out quantum linear algebra. The qubit count expo-4nentially compresses the Hilbert space, thus there is an exponential savings to represent the state. In terms of gate counts, the cost is to implement the Hamiltonian as a block-encoding [25, 26], where the cost is related to the classical data that needs to be loaded and the resulting subnormalization of the Hamiltonian, i.e. 

C ∼ subnormalization cost × data (13) However, because the Hamiltonian only contains some fixed-body level of interactions (e.g. two-particle inter-actions) the amount of data that needs to be loaded is poly( N ), independent of mtot . Similarly, because the Hamiltonian is an effective Hamiltonian for mtot -particles (regardless of system size), then its subnor-malization (lower bounded by the largest singular value of the Hamiltonian) is expected to be poly( mtot ), thus we get the reduction from a N mtot +c classical cost to poly( mtot )poly( N ) quantum cost. Similarly, we can compare an equivalent operation with the full quantum simulation (either in first or second quantization) [9, 10]. In this case, the cost has a sim-ilar form, the amount of data to load to represent the two-particle interactions is similarly poly( N ). However, since the Hamiltonian is now describing N -particles, we expect the subnormalization to be poly( N ), thus giving a poly( N )/poly( mtot ) factor speedup. While we can tune these speedups to be arbitrarily large by making mtot or N arbitrarily large, in this work we will be interested in speedup under practical simula-tion settings. This means that both mtot and N must correspond to physically relevant problem sizes, while the absolute costs must also be in a reasonable range for an actual simulation. For material sizes we therefore consider N in the range of ∼ 1000 atoms. For the neu-tral BSE applications considered here, biexcitons have 

m = 2 electron–hole pairs ( mtot = 4), and triexcitons have m = 3 pairs ( mtot = 6). We mainly focus on the 

m = 3 triexcitonic case in our resource estimates below as such states are generated and studied in physical ex-periments [27, 28], and serve as a balance between clas-sical complexity and direct relevance in multi-excitonic physics.

C. Optimized resource estimates for BSE calculations 

We now obtain our best quantum resource estimates by further optimizing the computational settings for multi-exciton BSE calculations targeting 0 .1 eV resolution in the excitation energy on the 10 × 10 × 10 k-point mesh. We focus on minimizing the Toffoli count. Since we are working with a quasiparticle theory and few-body Hamil-tonians, it is reasonable to consider the THC error as incurred per electron. We thus adjust the conservative THC fitting criterion used above to achieve an MP2 er-ror of below 0 .005 eV per active electron, and use space-group adaptation. The corresponding relative-integral-error criterion for the screened interaction is described in Appendix E. Details of the extrapolated inputs and error conventions are given in Appendix G. Diamond has 48 point-group operations, giving an ideal Toffoli-optimized reduction of approximately √48 ≃ 6.9. At fixed THC ranks, space-group adaptation reduces the walk cost by a factor of 4 .0: the direct cen-tral tensor ˜ζW and exchange state preparation remain in the physical basis, while the STHC transforms add over-head. Space-group-adapted fitting also yields a modestly smaller subnormalization at comparable relative ERI er-ror: for m = 3 and Nk = 1000, the extrapolated value de-creases from 157 .3 to 126 .7 Eh, which we attribute to the improved optimization landscape. Together, these effects reduce the Toffoli count by a factor of 5 .0, to 4 .7 × 10 10 ,while the logical-qubit count remains 1 .7 × 10 4 to the displayed precision. Following [9], assuming each Toffoli gate requires around 25 μs, we obtain a total runtime of about 14 days (as opposed to an estimated 10 5 −10 6 years with existing full Hamiltonian simulation protocols). For MgO, BN, AlN, and CdSe, we combine the space-group-adapted walk costs with the subnormalizations from the fits without space-group adaptation. These fixed-subnormalization estimates yield Toffoli reductions of 2 .7–4 .0, as shown in Fig. 4; detailed values are pro-vided in Appendix G. Without space-group adaptation, diamond, MgO, and BN have similar costs, between 1 .9 × 10 11 and 2 .3 × 10 11 

Toffolis. These systems have similar basis sizes of 26–27 atomic orbitals per unit cell in the GTH-cc-pVDZ basis (Table II). AlN has 52 orbitals per unit cell in the same basis and correspondingly higher requirements, at 3 .8 ×

V. CONCLUSIONS 

We have presented a detailed quasiparticle quantum simulation framework for multi-excitonic states within the Bethe-Salpeter equation. Our analysis of scaling ad-vantage versus standard full Hamiltonian quantum sim-ulation reveals up to 6th power asymptotic speedup in Toffoli gates and up to an exponential reduction in qubit count for quantum phase estimation. We further de-scribed an explicit first-quantized, orbital-based, block-encoding compilation and carried out detailed resource estimates for the classically challenging, but physically relevant, problem of tri-excitonic eigenvalue estimation across a range of semiconductor models. For the diamond benchmark system, at a realistic Brillouin zone sampling of 10 ×10 ×10, we achieve a 10 6 −10 7 reduction in Toffoli gate count versus standard full Hamiltonian simulation, reducing the estimated quantum run time from 10 4–10 5

years to roughly two weeks. Overall, this work illustrates the importance of existing classical heuristic frameworks in the era of fault-tolerant quantum simulation. The applicability of the heuristic approximations is to the same physical problem domains as studied in the classical simulations; however, quantum algorithms allow the possibility of extending the prob-lem domains in practice, for example, as pursued here in the description of multi-excitonic as opposed to single-excitonic solutions of the Bethe-Salpeter equation. This supports a broader thesis that existing classical heuristic ideas contain essential information that should be utilized in simulations of the physical world, and that designing quantum algorithms in the regimes where classical heuris-tics work [21] will be an essential direction in the further development of the field.

> 1. QROM and QROAM data-loading

All primitives will consist of a sequence of coherent data-loading of rotation angles, which are fed into multi-controlled rotation gates. Our primary data-loading primitive is quantum read-only memory (QROM) and its im-proved version QROAM [42]. Given a table of L entries f (j) ∈ { 0, 1}b, QROM implements the coherent map 

|j⟩| 0⟩⊗b 7 −→ | j⟩| f (j)⟩. Ref. [41] introduced unary-iteration QROM, which uses exactly L − 1 Toffolis. Ref. [47] introduced the Select–Swap network, enabling a Toffoli–ancilla trade-off that reduces the forward cost to O(√bL ). Ref. [42] introduced QROAM, which uses measurement and classical feedforward to uncompute the data registers at cost O(√L) independent of the word size b. In what follows, QROAM uses unary-iteration QROM as a subroutine, and all lookup costs include one external control. In our applications, the set of valid addresses is often a subset S ⊆ { 0, . . . , 2n − 1} (e.g., addresses distributed across padded blocks).
