# An Efficient Quantum Spectral-Galerkin Method for Linear Elliptic Equations 

Dongjie Wang 1 and Sheng Chen 2

> 1School of Mathematical Sciences, Beijing Normal University, Beijing, 100875, China
> 2Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Arts and Sciences, Beijing Normal University, Zhuhai, China

This paper presents an efficient quantum spectral-Galerkin method for constant-coefficient second-order linear elliptic equations. The method em-ploys a boundary-adapted Jacobi tensor-product basis that incorporates the boundary condition into the approximation space and produces Galerkin sys-tems with polynomially bounded condition numbers and sparsity. For analytic solutions satisfying a suitable uniform regularity condition, spectral conver-gence allows the normalized Galerkin coefficient state to be prepared with complexity poly( d, log(1 /ϵ )) , where d is the spatial dimension and ϵ is the error tolerance, under standard sparse-access and state-preparation assump-tions. At the state-preparation level, this improves the precision dependence over fixed-order finite-difference and finite-element discretizations. We further show how to estimate prescribed linear functionals of the solution, with an additional O(1 /ϵ ) factor arising from norm and inner-product estimation.

# 1 Introduction 

Partial differential equations (PDEs) provide fundamental mathematical models for diffu-sion, elasticity, electromagnetism, fluid mechanics, and quantum mechanics [1–5]. Despite their ubiquity, their numerical solution remains computationally challenging, especially in high dimensions. Two central difficulties are the curse of dimensionality [6] and the cost of high-precision approximation. For a d-dimensional problem discretized with N degrees of freedom per coordinate direction, the total number of unknowns scales as N d, so even storing the full solution vector requires resources exponential in d. Moreover, for low-order discretization schemes, the approximation error typically decays only algebraically with 

N ; hence achieving accuracy ϵ requires N = poly(1 /ϵ ) before the cost of solving the linear system is considered. Quantum computation offers a fundamentally different representation. Instead of stor-ing all entries of a discretized solution vector, a quantum algorithm may prepare a nor-malized state whose amplitudes encode the solution coefficients. For a tensor-product discretization with N one-dimensional degrees of freedom in each coordinate direction, 

1arXiv:2610.33095v1 [physics.comp-ph] 22 Oct 2026 the coefficient vector has length N d, whereas the corresponding quantum state requires only O(d log N ) qubits. This compact representation is one of the main motivations for quantum algorithms for PDEs. At the same time, it changes the computational objec-tive: reading out many individual entries may be costly, so one typically extracts global quantities such as expectation values or linear functionals of the solution [7]. Quantum linear systems algorithms (QLSAs) provide a natural route from PDE dis-cretizations to quantum algorithms [8, 9]. Many discretizations of linear PDEs reduce the computation of the expansion coefficients of an approximate solution to solving a finite-dimensional linear system Lc = f . Given suitable access to L and f , a QLSA can prepare a quantum state proportional to the coefficient vector c. The efficiency of this approach is governed by three basic quantities: the approximation error of the dis-cretization, the condition number κ(L), and the sparsity s(L). The approximation error determines how large the discretization parameter N must be to reach a prescribed tol-erance ϵ; the condition number controls the complexity and stability of the linear-system step; and the sparsity controls the cost of sparse matrix access. Therefore, a high-precision discretization suitable for QLSA-based algorithms should ideally combine spectral accu-racy, N = poly(log(1 /ϵ )) , with polynomially bounded condition number and sparsity, 

κ(L) = poly( N, d ) and s(L) = poly( N, d ).

1.1 Related work 

Several quantum frameworks for solving differential equations have been developed that are conceptually distinct from QLSA-based approaches. Schr¨ odingerization reformulates general linear differential equations as Schr¨ odinger-type dynamics through a warped phase transformation, thereby enabling their solution by Hamiltonian simulation [10]. Another approach is the linear combination of Hamiltonian simulation (LCHS), which represents nonunitary dynamics as a linear combination of Hamiltonian simulations without relying on a reduction to a dilated linear-system problem [11]. These approaches provide alterna-tive routes to quantum differential equation solvers. In the following discussion of quantum PDE algorithms, we focus on QLSA-based methods, which are most directly related to the framework developed in this work. 

QLSA-based quantum algorithms for PDEs. Low-order discretizations such as fi-nite difference and finite element methods are well suited to sparse linear algebra, and quantum algorithms based on these discretizations have been studied in several works. For finite difference methods, quantum algorithms and explicit block encodings have been developed for various elliptic boundary value problems [12, 13]. For finite element meth-ods, Montanaro and Pallister [7] analyzed quantum algorithms for estimating functionals of PDE solutions, building on classical finite element conditioning estimates such as those in [14]. These approaches naturally produce sparse and structured linear systems. How-ever, for fixed-order finite difference and finite element schemes, the approximation error decays only algebraically with the discretization parameter N . Consequently, achieving accuracy ϵ generally requires N = poly(1 /ϵ ), so the resulting quantum complexity retains a polynomial dependence on 1/ϵ .Spectral methods offer a natural way to overcome the algebraic accuracy barrier. For smooth or analytic solutions, they can converge exponentially fast, so high accuracy may be achieved with only N = poly(log(1 /ϵ )) degrees of freedom per coordinate direction [15]. This makes spectral discretizations particularly attractive for high-precision quan-tum algorithms. However, extending quantum spectral methods to non-periodic bound-ary conditions introduces additional structural challenges. The Chebyshev pseudospectral 

2method in [12] exploits the high-order convergence of Chebyshev expansions for Dirich-let problems, but the resulting differentiation matrices are generally non-Hermitian and non-normal. Consequently, their QLSA-relevant two-norm condition number is not de-termined by eigenvalue information alone, and analyzing it requires additional care. Arecent alternative imposes Dirichlet boundary conditions through an antisymmetric ex-tension followed by a Fourier-type periodic spectral method [16]. This approach requires additional boundary compatibility conditions, including the vanishing of the relevant even-order derivatives at the boundary, which are not generally satisfied for a generic Dirichlet problem. Thus, obtaining a quantum spectral discretization that is simultaneously high-precision, well-conditioned, and sparse remains nontrivial. 

Classical spectral-Galerkin methods. On the classical side, spectral-Galerkin meth-ods based on Jacobi and Legendre polynomials are well established [15, 17]. Shen [17] introduced efficient Legendre-Galerkin bases for second- and fourth-order problems using difference bases such as Lk+2 − Lk. These constructions retain spectral accuracy and lead to efficient one-dimensional discretizations. In multidimensional tensor-product settings, however, the presence of a nontrivial mass matrix can substantially increase the sparsity of the resulting system matrix and may also deteriorate its conditioning as the dimension grows. These features are less favorable for QLSA-based algorithms, whose complexity depends directly on the sparsity and condition number of the linear system. 

Summary of gaps. Low-order methods provide sparse linear systems but only algebraic accuracy, while existing spectral approaches face additional challenges in conditioning, boundary treatment, or high-dimensional tensor-product structure. This motivates the search for a spectral discretization that combines spectral accuracy with polynomially bounded condition number and sparsity. 

1.2 Our contributions 

In this paper, we propose a quantum spectral-Galerkin method for d-dimensional constant-coefficient second-order linear elliptic boundary value problems with homogeneous Dirich-let boundary conditions. Rather than building the quantum algorithm on a collocation or pseudospectral discretization, we work with the weak formulation and construct a Dirichlet-compatible Jacobi-based spectral basis. The one-dimensional basis functions are 

L2-orthonormal and vanish at the boundary, so the mass matrix is the identity, thereby avoiding the exponential-in-d sparsity growth that can arise from nontrivial mass matri-ces in tensor-product spectral discretizations. The d-dimensional approximation space is obtained by taking tensor products of these basis functions, thereby incorporating the boundary condition directly into the approximation space. This construction leads to symmetric positive-definite Galerkin systems whose con-dition numbers can be controlled through energy estimates. For analytic solutions sat-isfying a suitable uniform regularity condition, we prove spectral convergence, so that 

N = poly(log(1 /ϵ )) degrees of freedom per coordinate direction are sufficient. At the alge-braic level, the Galerkin matrix satisfies κ(L) = O(κ(A)N 4) and s(L) = O(d2N 2), where 

A denotes the constant symmetric positive-definite coefficient matrix of the elliptic oper-ator; when A is diagonal, the sparsity improves to O(dN ). More generally, we observe that the underlying sparsity and conditioning mechanisms are not specific to homoge-neous Dirichlet boundary conditions: they extend to other boundary conditions whenever a suitable L2-orthonormal polynomial basis is available, the associated variational prob-lem remains coercive, and the corresponding approximation space satisfies appropriate

2.2 Sparse-input QLSA and algorithmic objectives 

A numerical discretization of (2.1) leads to a finite-dimensional linear system. # 4 An L2-orthonormal Jacobi-based spectral-Galerkin method 

Motivated by the difficulties identified in Section 3.2, we now introduce a Jacobi-based spectral-Galerkin method for (2.1) . The proposed basis incorporates the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition directly while preserving orthonormality in the standard L2

inner product. We show that the resulting discretization has a condition number and sparsity polynomial in N and d, while spectral accuracy is obtained for analytic solutions satisfying a suitable uniform regularity condition. We first present the Jacobi-based basis construction in Section 4.1 and establish its main approximation, condition number, and sparsity properties in Lemma 4. We then introduce a rotated Jacobi-based basis in Section 4.2, which preserves the approximation space and condition number while further reducing the sparsity of the coefficient matrix. Finally, in Section 4.3, we analyze the classical complexity of solving the resulting linear systems by the conjugate gradient method. Basic properties of Jacobi polynomials used in the analysis are collected in Appendix B, while a concrete example and numerical results are presented in Appendix D.

Consequently, even if these one-dimensional matrices are dense, the sparsity of the multidimensional matrix remains polynomial in N and d.Moreover, provided that the corresponding variational problem is coercive and the poly-nomial approximation space satisfies appropriate inverse estimates, the same energy argu-ment as above yields a polynomial condition-number bound. If, in addition, the approxi-mation space retains spectral approximation properties, the main mechanism of the present spectral-Galerkin method extends to more general boundary conditions.

# 5 Quantum spectral-Galerkin method 

In this section, we present a quantum algorithm for solving the spectral-Galerkin linear system (3.8) . # 6 Conclusion and open problems 

In this paper, we presented a quantum spectral-Galerkin method for the d-dimensional second-order linear elliptic boundary value problem (2.1) with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. By combining Jacobi-based tensor-product bases, including a ro-tated variant, with a sparse-input QLSA, we obtained Galerkin coefficient matrices whose sparsity and condition number grow only polynomially with the discretization parameter and the dimension. As a result, for analytic solutions satisfying the uniform regularity con-dition (4.22) , the complexity of preparing the quantum state associated with the Galerkin coefficient vector is poly( d, log(1 /ϵ )) , improving the precision dependence over fixed-order finite-difference and finite-element discretizations, where we suppress the additional de-pendence on κ(A), Cd, and lower-order logarithmic factors. When the output is taken to be a linear functional of the solution, the overall complexity acquires an additional factor 

1/ϵ through norm estimation and Hadamard-test-based estimation. Our analysis leaves several natural questions for future work. 1. State preparation for general data. State preparation remains a major bottleneck. As in the existing literature on quantum finite element methods [7, 24, 25], we assume that the relevant input states |f ⟩ and |ρ⟩ can be prepared efficiently. For general right-hand sides and observables, however, it remains unclear when this assumption is justified. A better understanding of the structural conditions that enable efficient state preparation for PDE-related data is therefore an important direction for future work. 2. Variable-coefficient problems. It is natural to ask whether the present framework can be extended to variable-coefficient equations. In this setting, the simple tensor-product structure exploited in the present analysis is generally lost, potentially lead-ing to denser matrices and less favorable condition numbers. A possible approach is to use the constant-coefficient part as a preconditioner, although it remains unclear whether the resulting preconditioned system admits an efficient sparse representation and quantum implementation. 3. Weaker regularity assumptions and broader PDE classes. The complexity of our algorithm depends on the regularity quantity Cd in (4.22) , which encodes the size of certain high-order derivatives of the solution. It is natural to ask whether similar quantum complexity guarantees can be obtained under weaker regularity assump-tions or without explicit dependence on such high-order derivative information. More broadly, one may ask whether the ideas developed here can be extended to other classes of PDEs, such as convection-diffusion equations, time-dependent equations, higher-order elliptic equations, or nonlinear problems. 

23 These questions touch on fundamental structural and analytical constraints that deter-mine whether a spectral discretization is compatible with efficient quantum linear algebra. Answering them will help clarify the ultimate scope of quantum spectral methods for partial differential equations.
