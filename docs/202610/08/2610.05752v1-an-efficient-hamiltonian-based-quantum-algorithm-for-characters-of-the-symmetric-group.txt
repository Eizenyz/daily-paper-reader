# An efficient Hamiltonian-based quantum algorithm for characters of the symmetric group

Abstract 

Consider a quantum state vector proportional to any given column of the character table of the symmetric group ( Sn), corresponding to a superposition over the irreps weighted by the character values. It was recently argued that sampling from this state is classically hard under reasonable complexity-theoretic assumptions, while there exists an efficient quantum algorithm for preparing this character state using the quantum Fourier transform (QFT) over Sn [1]. We give an alternative, simpler algorithm to prepare this character state with a sequence of Hamil-tonian evolutions, as well as an efficient implementation with reconfigurable qubits that uses only nearest-neighbor gates in 1D. The gate complexity, accounting for Hamiltonian simulation errors, depends on the choice of the column: For fixed target state error, the estimated suf-ficient total gate count ranges from as few as O(n) gates for the n-cycle column, to eO(n1.75 )gates near the conjecturally classically hard parameter regime, to provably eO(n2.5) gates in the worst case. In contrast, the QFT subroutine in the prior approach uses eO(n3) gates. Our gate count estimates come from leading-order state-dependent Trotter error analysis, and the proven worst-case bound relies on a QSVT-based Hamiltonian simulation method; numerical bench-marks of the Trotter-based protocol suggest that the estimates are conservative. Furthermore, we generalize our protocol to give an explicit algorithm for the quantum character transform (QCT) over Sn using eO(n2.5) gates and O(n) qubits. An application to entanglement entropy of symmetric orbifold conformal field theories is also discussed.

Symmetry plays a central role in the description and simulation of quantum many-body systems. It allows us to resolve the Hilbert space of a system into its irreducible symmetry sectors. For a symmetry group G, its characters are the fundamental quantities that connect these sectors to the action of its group elements. Here we focus on the symmetric group Sn consisting of the n! permutations of n objects. Its conjugacy classes, labeled by cycle types, classify permutations up to relabeling. Its irreducible representations (irreps), labeled by Young diagrams, describe the possible symmetry sectors of the system. The normalized character table can be viewed as a Fourier transform between conjugacy classes and irreducible representations within the space of class functions. In many-body physics, Sn governs the exchange of identical particles, with its irreps organizing the spin and spatial symmetry sectors of quantum gases. It also appears in replica methods, where 

Sn permutes the replicas introduced to study disordered systems and many-body entanglement [2,3]. Moreover, the characters of Sn determine the Slater-basis amplitudes of Laughlin states in the fractional quantum Hall effect [4]. A concrete setting where characters of Sn have a physical interpretation is in the two-dimensional symmetric orbifold conformal field theories (CFTs). A symmetric orbifold CFT is formed from n

identical copies of a conformal field theory by gauging the Sn symmetry that permutes them [5]. The gauged theory has a generalized symmetry realized by topological line operators (defects) labeled by irreducible representations of Sn [6, 7]. The action of these defects on twist operators is determined by the characters of Sn. Recent work has shown that the entanglement entropy in the presence of these defects can be computed directly from these characters [8]. Using QSVT for the Hamiltonian simulation subroutine, our algorithm uses eO(n5/2) gates and O(n)qubits both for CSP in the worst case (Theorem 1.3) and for the full QCT (Theorem 1.4). We also propose an efficient Trotter-based implementation that is particularly suitable for re-configurable qubits in 1D or 2D geometry (e.g., neutral atom arrays [15]). The proposed im-plementation leverages the fast fermionic permutation protocol with reconfigurable qubits from Maskara et al. [16], which makes the Hamiltonian simulation gates nearest-neighbor. For exam-ple, the required fermionic permutation can be implemented as a 2D reflection with O(n) gates. At the Θ( √n/ log n)-cycle regime proposed as a candidate for quantum advantage in character sampling [1], our leading-order state-dependent Trotter error analysis for uniform cycles ν = ( sm)suggests that |χν ⟩ can be prepared with eO(n7/4/δ ) gates, where δ is the target state error (The-orem 1.2). As a complementary result, we give a classical algorithm to sample from Pν (λ) that is efficient when ν = ( sm) is exactly uniform, but becomes inefficient under perturbations from uniformity (Theorem 1.5). Our numerical simulations of the Trotterized CSP circuit up to n = 48 suggest that the true gate count needed for a fixed state error changes modestly when a uniform cycle type is perturbed, and the analytical bounds overestimate the count for many cycle types by various powers of n. Hence, we believe this Trotter-based implementation of CSP is a promising candidate to realize near-term quantum advantage with reconfigurable qubits.

Classically tractable cycle types. We are interested in the potential of our efficient CSP/QCT algorithms for realizing quantum advantage by sampling from |χν ⟩. The Bravyi et al. argument for quantum advantage requires cycle types ν in the regime (1.7), which ensures granularity but not average-case hardness. We show that the number of cycles alone does not determine classical hardness due to the following classical algorithm: 

Theorem 1.5 (Classical samplers; see Theorems 6.1 and 6.2) . For uniform cycles ν = ( sm), the distribution Pν of (1.3) can be sampled exactly by a classical algorithm in expected time eO(n2),and each probability Pν (λ) can be computed exactly in polynomial time. For almost-uniform cycles 

ν = ( sm, r 1, . . . , r b), an exact rejection sampler runs in expected time n4b+O(1) , and each probability can be computed in time n2b+O(1) .

The uniform-cycle sampler is built from the s-quotient of λ: the sizes of its s components are drawn from a multinomial distribution, and each component from the Plancherel measure by Robinson-Schensted insertion of a random permutation. Uniform cycles with Θ( √n/ log n)cycles lie inside the regime (1.7) and are nevertheless classically tractable, as are perturbations of them with a constant number of exceptional cycles. Any superpolynomial quantum advantage in this neighborhood must therefore come from a number of exceptional cycles that grows with 

n. We do not have a state-dependent Trotter bound for such cycle types; nevertheless, numerical benchmarking up to n = 48 suggests that the required Trotter steps change only modestly with the number of exceptional cycles (see Figure 6). 

Application: entanglement entropy in CFTs. Applying the inverse QCT to an irreducible representation |λ⟩ and measuring returns a cycle type ν with probability Pλ(ν) = [ χλ(ν)] 2/z ν ,which is the weight appearing in the defect entropy (1.1). Section 5.2 shows that samples of ν can be used to estimate ∆ Sλ with additive error at most ln p(n) ∼ O(√n), where p(n) is the number of partitions of integer n. On the other hand, numerical evaluations of ∆ Sλ for all possible λ up to n = 30 suggest that typical values of |∆Sλ| exceed ln p(n) at large n, and the growth of its magnitude is consistent with Θ( n) (see Figure 8). This suggests that for a generic λ, our quantum algorithm gives an efficient estimator of ∆ Sλ with relative error that vanishes with n.

4.2 Implementation via quantum singular value transformation 

The Trotter construction has three limitations: its dependence on the target error is inverse-linear, its worst-case bound is O(n4 log 2 n/δ ), and its state-dependent improvement is a leading-order bound for uniform cycles only. We now replace each Trotterized transfer with QSVT-based Hamiltonian simulation, which gives a complete bound of eO(n5/2) gates for every cycle type with polylogarithmic dependence on the error. We first construct an explicit block-encoding of Hs, then bound the cost of CSP for a fixed cycle type, and then extend the construction coherently over all cycle types. Section 5.1 completes the QCT by erasing the cycle-type registers. We assume a gate set of arbitrary one- and two-qubit gates. Constant-arity controlled gates are decomposed at constant cost. Arithmetic and comparisons on O(log n) qubits are implemented reversibly. Compilation into a fixed discrete gate set introduces additional polylogarithmic precision factors. The gate counts below are circuit-size bounds. We assume throughout that 0 < δ < 1.

# 7 Discussion and Outlook 

We have given a Hamiltonian-based algorithm for character state preparation, and extended it coherently to realize the quantum character transform over Sn. Our construction replaces the existing quantum Fourier transform-based approach [1] by a sequence of transfer evolutions under the Hamiltonians Hs, each run for an exactly known time. Simulated using QSVT, the resulting circuit uses eO(n5/2 log δ−1) gates and O(n) qubits for CSP at any cycle type and for the full QCT. This is below the eO(n3) gates and eO(n3/2) qubits of the best known implementation [14] of the quantum Fourier transform over Sn.Towards near-term hardware-friendly realizations, we also consider implementing the Hamil-tonian evolution subroutines of our algorithm by Trotter decomposition. Using fast fermionic permutations with reconfigurable qubits [16], the Hamiltonian evolution can be implemented with geometrically local gates in 1D or 2D geometry. While the general operator-norm Trotter error bound indicates a gate complexity of eO(n4/δ ), we improved the bound by focusing on the states that our algorithm actually visits. Looking at the leading-order Trotter error, our CSP algorithm has a sufficient gate complexity of eO(n7/4/δ ) for uniform cycles with the Θ( √n/ log n) cycle count that the hardness argument of Bravyi et al. [1] requires for the granularity condition. To better 38 understand classical hardness, we showed that such uniform cycle types, as well as their perturba-tions by a constant number of exceptional cycles, admit exact polynomial-time classical samplers despite satisfying the granularity condition. Nevertheless, when the number of exceptional cycles grows with n, or for generic cycle types with Θ( √n/ log n) cycles, no efficient classical algorithms are known, and quantum advantage is still possible. Our numerical simulations up to n = 48 suggest that the Trotter method remains efficient for these general cycle types, and generally have far better resource scaling than what our bounds predict. These results motivate further study of the Trotter-based CSP, which we put forth as a candidate quantum advantage task on near-term reconfigurable-qubit quantum hardware. Several interesting questions remain open: 

• Can the full Trotter error on the states visited by the algorithm be bounded, beyond the leading-order term? Our state-dependent bound controls only the error at order in 1 /M ,where M is the number of Trotter steps. A bound on the full error would turn our eO(n7/4/δ )estimate into a rigorous complexity bound. It would also be valuable to extend the analysis to cycle types with a growing number of exceptional cycles, where known classical algorithms become inefficient. For these cycle types, our numerics show smaller costs than both the worst-case bounds and the estimated bounds from the state-dependent error analysis, suggesting there is much room for improvements. 

• Can our Hamiltonian transfer mechanism also prepare the Kostka and Littlewood-Richardson states described in Crichigno and Prakash [11]? These quantum spin-chain states encode Kostka numbers and Littlewood–Richardson coefficients as amplitudes, with the currents replaced by noncommutative complete symmetric polynomials and noncommutative Schur polynomials. Bravyi et al. [1] turned the complete symmetric operators into a classical MPS algorithm for Kostka numbers and left the Littlewood-Richardson case open. Neither work gives a quantum algorithm for preparing these states. Larocca and Havl´ ıˇ cek [23] compute Kostka, Littlewood-Richardson, plethysm and Kronecker coefficients with QFT-based quan-tum algorithms whenever the ratio of the relevant representation dimensions is polynomial. In this regime, they give an efficient classical algorithm for Kostka numbers and conjecture one for Littlewood-Richardson coefficients. For Kronecker coefficients, a classical algorithm of Panova [24] reduces the conjectured advantage to a polynomial gap. Exact Hamiltonian transfers for these nonunitary operators would give a QFT-free route to the same quantities. 

• Can we generalize our CSP and QCT algorithms to other finite groups? In particular, QCT for general finite groups has applications in symmetry-adapted quantum simulations [13]. The transfer mechanism (Lemma 3.1) can be applied to any family of linear operators with the ladder property of Proposition 3.2, so groups whose characters obey a Murnaghan-Nakayama-type rule, such as the wreath products Zk ≀ Sn, are natural candidates. 

• Can our Hamiltonian-based approach for the QCT be extended to realize the full quantum Fourier transform over Sn? The QCT is the restriction of the QFT to class functions, with the representation-matrix indices traced out. It would be useful to investigate whether the Hamiltonian approach can reach the remaining O(n log n) qubits of the Fourier basis at a cost below the eO(n3) gates in the state-of-the-art QFT circuits [14]. 39 Acknowledgments
