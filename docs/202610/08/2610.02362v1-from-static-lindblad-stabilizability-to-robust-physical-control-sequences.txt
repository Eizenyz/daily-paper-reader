Dissipative dynamics can stabilize a quantum target without providing a reliable sequence of finite control operations. We derive checkable conditions for implementing such stabilization with imperfect controls, with bounds on convergence, errors during operations, and physical cost. The analysis includes pulses, idle intervals, and all simultaneously acting background dynamics. Using a common Lyapunov measure of deviation from the target, we require a contraction bound that remains uniformly below one over the prescribed uncertainty set after accounting for implementation errors. If all allowed paths preserve the target subspace throughout each operation, repeated cycles converge to that subspace from every initial state, even when errors vary between cycles. Comparison with these certified target-preserving paths gives an explicit long-time error bound under bounded leakage. Two single-qubit counterexamples show why a stabilizing average or a stabilizing generator obtained by unitary conjugation does not ensure these path conditions. The certification approach also supports a finite-library search. For a specified four-level cascade model with finite pulses, calibration and timing errors, dephasing, and bounded pumping, this search yields a sequence with an explicit preparation-time bound. For every initial state, it guarantees trace distance at most 0 .1from the target at the end of preparation and throughout subsequent cycles. 

I. INTRODUCTION 

Reservoir engineering asks for dynamics that drive every initial state toward a prescribed quantum state or code space. Laser-controlled reservoir design was proposed as a way to select a motional master equation [1], and designed dissipation has been developed for steady-state quantum computation and correlated-state preparation [2]. Experi-ments have combined coherent gates with optical pumping to realize dissipative entangled-state preparation in trapped ions [3], and continuous drives with an engineered reservoir to autonomously stabilize a Bell state in superconducting circuits [4]. These developments make the passage from a generator-level design to an executable protocol a practical mathematical question. 

A. Physical problem 

For a time-independent master equation, the question is geometric: does the available cone contain a Gorini– Kossakowski–Lindblad–Sudarshan (GKLS) generator with the required invariant target and stable transverse dynam-ics? A laboratory asks which calibrated controls are applied, for how long, what acts during every transition, and how much time and control are required. The generator cone forgets the rate assigned to a ray, whether a sum is directly callable, and the path and cost of a conjugating pulse. We call one complete finite-duration hold, pulse, transition, dead interval, or cooldown a primitive ; its generator contains every simultaneous always-on term. A physical word is a finite chronological sequence of such primitives. Its intermediate action cannot generally cancel in the way a formal generator sum does. The target face consists of the density operators supported on the target subspace. A static Lyapunov certificate specifies a positive-definite metric and a decay margin for deviations from the target. A 

target-compatible atomization expresses the certified generator as a finite positive sum of callable complete generators, each preserving the target. A word grammar specifies allowed finite chronological primitive templates and their parameter ranges. For a supplied template, the nominal synthesis uses ideal factors to approximate the static evolution. Each primitive also has a complete reference path , containing all simultaneous dynamics at its reference settings, and admissible actual paths over the uncertainty family. > B. Main results

Two obstructions show why the middle interfaces are substantive. First, two directly available qubit generators have an average equal to amplitude damping toward a pure target, while the first positive hold under either generator makes that target full rank. No later finite-time Markovian factor can return the state to the pure target. Static global asymptotic stability therefore does not imply even stroboscopic stabilization, meaning stabilization at the chosen cycle boundaries. Second, a bit flip conjugates an excitation generator into target-stabilizing amplitude damping, but the corresponding bounded pulse moves the target to the orthogonal state. With switchable excitation the repeated protocol approaches a nonconstant periodic orbit; with unavoidable excitation it cannot return to the target even stroboscopically. Algebraic orbit access does not by itself specify a target-preserving physical path. For a supplied finite word grammar, the positive theorem gives a compiler : a construction of contraction, within-cycle, and resource bounds from the static certificate, nominal-synthesis bounds, and complete-path data. Robust 3means uniform over a declared compact uncertainty family. Its members may vary arbitrarily from one cycle to the next, with any correlations admitted by that family. All complete words begin and end at a reproducible cold boundary ,a fully specified controller state at which cycles concatenate. In one fixed Lyapunov metric, normalized-time Duhamel bounds convert complete generator and duration uncertainty into primitive block errors. A fully amplified product telescope combines these errors with a nominal-synthesis bound and the static decay. The resulting quantity qrob 

bounds every actual monodromy; the condition qrob < 1 certifies uniform contraction. The same primitive envelopes produce a within-cycle bound Brob , hence all-time convergence and componentwise worst-case resources. The conclusion depends on the physical meaning of the uncertainty. In the structure-preserving class, every admis-sible GKLS path preserves the target face at every time. The exact theorem then gives all-time attraction to that face; for a rank-one face this is stabilization of a fixed pure state. A general small generator perturbation may leak from the face. For this leaky class, complete pathwise diamond-norm bounds relative to structure-preserving shadow paths produce cycle and prefix leakage estimates. Iteration gives a practical tube with an explicit asymptotic radius. Two analytically solvable three-level models provide explicit control words and resource bounds. A swap confined to the excited subspace turns one common-ground decay into the other without moving the ground target. Its four-primitive word gives uniform contraction under the declared relative gain errors in the two holds, both without dephasing and with known always-on dephasing. A distinct cascade system has an exact three-factor identity and, in its stated two-label control alphabet, a matching lower bound with sharp leading wall time 4 log(1 /ε ) as the target-error tolerance ε ↓ 0. The latter constant belongs to that alphabet and accounting convention and is not transferred to the common-ground word. A second construction uses a complete-path certificate directly as the acceptance test for a finite search. It can use the preceding Lyapunov bounds or a model-specific positive observable, such as the population outside a pure target. This permits a distinction between certifying a supplied word and selecting one from a declared library. The four-level example below uses the latter construction with a survival-probability certificate. The four-level model tests a different part of the construction. It combines an irreversible cascade with finite pulses whose amplitude and duration are uncertain, always-on dephasing, and bounded pumping out of the target. A four-coordinate exact adjoint reduction retains pulse-induced coherences and encloses the survival probability over the whole error box for every initial state. A search over 1650 parameterized words finds a five-command sequence that guarantees trace distance at most 0 .1 after a complete preparation cycle ending by time 14 .3107 and throughout subsequent cycles. All times use inverse nominal cascade-rate units. This is a sufficient execution-time bound for a stated hardware model. A periodic-grid comparison and proposals from ordinary numerical optimization use the same rational acceptance test. Definition 2 (Calibrated physical presentation) A calibrated presentation is a finite collection of primitive labels and a finite directed graph of controller states. A label identifies one complete operating point: its generator or channel path, physical rate and amplitude range, admissible duration, concurrency rule, and cost vector. A graph vertex records every controller variable needed to repeat the experiment, including the laboratory frame, phase when relevant, actuator configuration, and environment/reset state. Algebraically equal superoperators remain different labels when their physical invocations differ.

A signature covers the complete prescribed waveform and its admitted uncertainty family. The idle setting, denoted IDLE, and distinct directed pulse commands have separate signatures. > V. ROBUST COMPILATION FROM PRIMITIVE PHYSICAL PATHS

An orbit identity describes a relation in operator space, not the controller path that realizes it. Bounded Eulerian control replaces impulsive group jumps by continuous paths [11]; in soft-pulse Markovian models, the control Hamil-tonian and dissipation act simultaneously during the pulse [12]. The complete paths are therefore part of the input to the physical-word theorem.

> VII. SEARCHING A FINITE HARDWARE LIBRARY A. Acceptance and refinement in a finite library

A complete path certificate can also serve as the acceptance test in a search over executable words. Switched dissipative stabilization and robust open-system performance already have substantial theory [5, 18]. The question here is narrower: which words in a specified hardware library have a verified guarantee for their full uncertainty set, including the physical pulses and intervening idle dynamics? Fix a finite operation alphabet and a maximal word length. For each word, use a compact parameter domain whose coordinates enforce all exact target and controller identities. The uncertainty set covers every admitted joint error, including correlations between operations. The system model includes every quantum degree of freedom retained between calls. Each nonempty cycle has duration between fixed positive finite bounds. These choices specify the domain on which a search result is meaningful.

> VIII. FINITE-INPUT AND MARKOVIAN SCOPE

The compiler is finite-dimensional and its quantitative output remains tied to the supplied presentation. The following examples identify distinct mathematical limits of that input class. 

a. Finite support does not control timing. For the common-ground family 

Lδ = D[|0⟩⟨ 1|] + δD[|0⟩⟨ 2|], δ = 2 −n, n ∈ N, (106) the dimension and support size remain fixed while the two direct weights are (1 , δ ). With unit rates and a common minimum dwell τ , the first-order schedule must satisfy 

hdwell min = τ /δ. (107) Thus finite Carathéodory support supplies neither a uniform positive lower bound on weights nor a dimension-only dwell guarantee. Optimizing the achieved margin min j λj /(rj τj ) requires its own compactness and calibration hy-potheses and still omits interstitial dynamics and the stability upper endpoint. 

b. A higher-rank target face. Let 

P = P0 + P1, AΛ = D[|0⟩⟨ 2|], BΛ = D[|1⟩⟨ 2|]. (108) Both generators preserve FP and have the same transverse action, although [ AΛ, B Λ]( P2) = P1 − P0. A swap within the target subspace conjugates one jump into the other along a face-preserving path. The transverse certificate proves attraction to FP ; it cannot select a unique mixed state because every state in the face is stationary under a single decay from level two. A unique target would require a separate contraction certificate for motion within the face. 

Remark 37 (Finite-instance and Markovian scope) For a fixed finite support, graph, word order, closed-form path library, and algebraic or rational enclosures, the compiler’s equalities, positivity conditions, timing inequalities, and integer cycle counts reduce to finite symbolic calculations that certify supplied data. Real quantifier elimination also decides a separately supplied finite first-order formula over the real algebraic numbers. These facts do not de-cide unbounded word length, unrestricted waveforms, generic matrix-exponential identities, or global minimum cost. Numerical search supplies candidates; failure to find a certificate establishes neither its nonexistence nor physical infeasibility without a necessary analytic obstruction. The physical-word theorem requires the declared system-channel factorization. If system–environment memory sur-vives a cut, the segment maps need not compose as CPTP channels and the theorem does not apply. Such dynamics requires a model that accounts for the surviving memory.  

> IX. CONCLUSION

A stabilizing Lindblad generator and an executable stabilization protocol are different mathematical objects. The generator belongs to a cone; the protocol is a timed, ordered path through calibrated operating points. The two 24 qubit obstructions isolate two independent losses of information: convex averaging can hide target leakage of every primitive, and a perfect orbit identity can hide a pulse path that moves the target or adds irreversible noise. The robust primitive-certified compiler bounds the physical cycle without replacing it by an effective generator. It combines static decay in one fixed Lyapunov metric, a finite nominal-word derivation, and normalized-clock Duhamel bounds for complete uncertain primitives. The amplified word telescope controls actual-monodromy contraction and within-cycle prefixes. Uniform slack gives all-time target-face attraction under cycle-varying uncertainty, with worst-case duration and componentwise resource bounds. Rank-one targets yield fixed-state stabilization; selecting one mixed state in a higher-rank face requires an additional intraface certificate. General generator perturbations need not preserve the target face. The leaky theorem therefore compares complete actual paths with structure-preserving shadow paths. Primitive diamond-norm bounds produce cycle and prefix leakage, an explicit practical tube, and the tolerance at which the certificate ceases to guarantee all-time accuracy. The common-ground qutrit realizes the fixed-metric construction with bounded directed pulses and uncertain decay gains. The separate cascade qutrit gives matching wall-time bounds with leading term 4 log(1 /ε ) in its two-label grammar. These examples connect the generator-level criterion to calibrated control paths and their execution costs. The finite-search extension uses these certificates to select executable words from a specified hardware library. The four-level cascade retains irreversible transfer, uncertain finite pulses, timing errors, and continuous background dynamics. Its survival-effect calculation certifies trace distance at most 0 .1 for arbitrary initial states after a complete preparation cycle ending by time 14 .3107 and throughout subsequent cycles. This direct observable calculation and the static-metric construction are two ways of supplying the acceptance test. The search finds strictly feasible certificates under its stated refinement assumptions and ranks certified execution-time bounds within its declared domain. Physical optimality and the computational cost of a general search require separate results.

The finite examples use exact rational and real algebraic arithmetic, together with certified rational enclosures for logarithms and exponentials. Static data. For a supplied certificate, validate the target projector, coherent basis, compact semialgebraic orbit descriptions, quotient-span dimension, and positive gauge. The exact branch checks the GKLS or Kraus target-face conditions; the leaky branch checks complete-generator diamond bounds relative to structure-preserving shadow paths. Normalized-clock logarithmic-norm and generator-difference bounds include duration error.
