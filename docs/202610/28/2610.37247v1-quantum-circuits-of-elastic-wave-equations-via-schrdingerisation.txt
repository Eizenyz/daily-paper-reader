Abstract 

We construct explicit quantum circuits for the velocity–stress formulation of elastic wave equations in homogeneous media using Schrödingerisation. After rewriting the equations in symmetric matrix form(SMF), we use Fourier spectral discretization for source-driven periodic problems and staggered-grid finite differences for Dirichlet, Neumann, and impedance bound-ary conditions. The Schrödingerisation method transforms any linear PDEs into a system of Schrödinger-type PDEs –with unitary evolution– using the warped phase transformation that maps the equations in one higher dimension. For periodic problems, we develop circuits by com-bining controlled Fourier-mode rotations. For physical boundaries, interleaved encoding swaps the spatial and field-component registers, enabling a more efficient circuit construction using shift operators. We analyze pth-order Trotter–Suzuki formulas and derive splitting-error and CNOT-gate estimates, under the assumptions of smooth auxiliary initialization and solution-state recovery. Comparison with classical partitioned splitting methods identifies a polyno-mial improvement in spatial-resolution dependence for the quantum state-evolution subroutine. Noiseless circuit simulations of a forced periodic problem and an impedance problem illustrate the constructions and quantify their errors against exact solutions and classical reference com-putations. 

Keywords : Elastic wave equations, quantum circuits, Schrödingerisation method 

# 1 Introduction 

Elastic wave equations describe the propagation of mechanical disturbances in solids [1] and are widely used in seismology and geodynamics [2]. The velocity–stress formulation gives a first-order hyperbolic system that is well suited to staggered-grid discretization and the treatment of physical

In practical problems, Dirichlet, Neumann, and impedance conditions model fixed, traction, and absorbing boundaries, while source terms represent external forces or seismic excitations [4]. Therefore, it is important to develop efficient algorithms for elastic systems with source terms and physically relevant boundary conditions. Classical numerical methods, such as finite difference methods [5], finite element methods [6], boundary element methods [7] and other numerical methods [8] , have been extensively developed for elastic wave simulation. Nevertheless, large-scale simulations in high dimensions, long-time wave propagation, and models with fine spatial resolution or complex media may still lead to substantial computational cost. This has motivated the development of quantum algorithms for partial differential equations. Early approaches mainly relied on quantum linear system algorithms, such as HHL-type methods, by discretizing the PDE and reformulating it as a linear system [9, 10]. More recently, Hamiltonian-simulation-based approaches have provided a more natural and advantageous framework for PDEs. The Schrödingerisationembeds general non-unitary dynamics into a unitary evolution in an enlarged Hilbert space [11,12], which is the only method that transfer general linear PDEs into unitary PDEs, thus also most natural for continuous quantum computing [13]. A complementary approach is the linear combination of Hamiltonian simulation (LCHS) method for simulating non-unitary dynamics [14]. Based on these frameworks, explicit quantum circuits have been developed for several classes of PDEs, including hyperbolic equations [15] and canonical PDEs in Fourier space [16]. These techniques have also been applied to elastic wave propagation, for which explicit quantum algorithms and circuit constructions have been investigated [17]. These developments demonstrate the importance of exploiting the algebraic and differential structure of PDEs to obtain implementable quantum circuits with explicit complexity estimates. A useful framework for connecting general linear PDEs with Hamiltonian simulation is Schrödin-gerisation method [11,12]. The main idea is to transform a non-Schrödinger or non-unitary evolution into an enlarged Schrödinger-type system with unitary evolution, so that Hamiltonian simulation techniques can be applied. The formulation is natural for PDEs and other realistic physical mod-els. Explicit Schrödingerisation circuits have been developed for advection and heat equations [18], Maxwell’s equations [19], Black–Scholes equations [20], and heat equations with physical bound-ary conditions [21]. Its application to elastic wave equations has also been studied [22]. For the velocity–stress system, the symmetric matrix form (SMF) [23, 24] exposes the coupling between physical components and spatial derivatives. The source-free periodic system admits unitary evo-lution after discretization, whereas forcing and physical boundary terms require a treatment of the resulting non-unitary dynamics. In this paper, we develop explicit circuits for these semidiscrete systems. We use Fourier spec-tral discretization under periodic boundary conditions and staggered-grid finite differences under physical boundary conditions. The circuits are assembled from elementary evolutions using nested product formulas. We further analyze the error contributions arising from the Hamiltonian simula-tion and the spatial and auxiliary-variable discretizations. Smooth auxiliary initialization [25] is used in the complexity analysis to control the auxiliary Fourier resolution. The main contributions are as follows: 2(a) We construct quantum circuits for periodic velocity–stress systems with time-independent sources. Fourier diagonalization gives controlled rotations for the spatial and auxiliary vari-ables. For d = 2 , 3, we also provide explicit circuit constructions and estimates of the corre-sponding CNOT-gate complexity. (b) We derive circuits for physical boundary conditions using exchanged encoding for the spatial and the field-component registers. The Neumann circuit follows by conjugating the Dirichlet spatial circuit, while the impedance construction uses exact controlled rotations on the two boundary subspaces. (c) We derive commutator-based error bounds and CNOT-gate estimates for pth-order Trotter– Suzuki formulas, including the effects of auxiliary approximation. Comparison with classical methods identifies a polynomial advantage in spatial resolution for the state-evolution sub-routine under the stated assumptions. (d) The circuits experiments are performed using the Unitarylab package [26], which facilitates the construction and execution of the quantum circuits on a local simulator. Each test compares the recovered circuit solution with the exact PDE solution, a classical partitioned solution, and a classical Schrödingerised solution. Our setting differs from Refs. [16,17] mainly in its modelling scope. Ref. [16] treats the isotropic acoustic wave equation, whereas we consider the coupled elastic velocity–stress system with both compressional and shear waves. Ref. [17] considers a source-free elastic system with a specific zero boundary treatment, while our framework, enabled by Schrödingerisation to embed the non-unitary dynamics into a unitary evolution, includes periodic conditions with sources and Dirichlet, Neumann, and impedance boundary conditions. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the velocity–stress equations and their symmetric matrix form. Section 3 introduces Schrödingerisation, recovery, and smooth auxiliary initialization. Section 4 develops the circuits of the source-driven system and their complexity estimates in one and multiple dimensions. Section 5 treats physical boundary conditions. Section 6 presents the numerical experiments, and Section 7 concludes the paper. Throughout this paper, ∥·∥ denotes the spectral norm. Remark 5.6. For fixed d, and when only the quantum state-evolution subroutine is considered, the staggered-grid Schrödingerisation algorithm retains a polynomial advantage in the spatial resolution over the corresponding classical method. Ignoring the logarithmic factors, the gate complexity has the same order as that of Ref. [17] which considers a centered finite-difference discretization under periodic boundary conditions. The present method accommodates Dirichlet, Neumann, and impedance boundary conditions. Moreover, the symmetric structure of the SMF further simplifies the circuit decomposition. In particular, the implementation of UI requires O(np) CNOT gates, where np = log 2 Np, rather than a cost depending directly on the auxiliary discretization size Np [18]. Thus, the proposed construction reduces the circuit-level overhead while preserving the same leading asymptotic complexity.

# 7 Conclusion 

We have developed explicit Schrödingerisation circuits for the velocity–stress elastic wave equa-tions in homogeneous media. The periodic construction combines Fourier spectral discretization with controlled rotations. For physical boundary conditions, staggered-grid discretization and in-terleaved encoding lead to shift circuits and boundary corrections. These constructions accommo-date time-independent source terms and non-unitary boundary dynamics within the same Hamilto-nian simulation framework. Our results establish a theoretical foundation for quantum-accelerated modeling–with polynomial advantage over its classical counterparts for quantum state computation– of elastic wave propagation, with potential applications in geophysical exploration and materials science. Realistic wave propagation often involves complex boundary value problems and unbounded domains. Our future efforts will focus on quantum algorithms for such generalized scenarios, derive 27 the corresponding quantum circuit for practical simulations and assess the costs of input preparation, observable estimation, and implementation with a finite gate set.
