## Pathwise Random Hamiltonian Simulation 

Davide Cugini 1

> 1

Dipartimento di Fisica, Universit` a di Pavia, via Bassi 6, 27100, Pavia, Italy 

Randomized product formulas such as qDrift offer a resource-efficient alternative to deterministic Trotter–Suzuki decompositions for Hamiltonian simulation, removing their polynomial dependence on the number of Hamiltonian terms. qDrift, however, is intrinsically limited to first order in the evolution time, so its query complexity remains linear in the inverse of the target accuracy, 1 /ϵ . We introduce Pathwise Random Hamiltonian Simulation (PRHS), which extends qDrift to arbitrary order by subdividing each time step into M correlated slices, each evolving under a term sampled from a quasi-probability distribution that we construct in closed form and prove unique, with a bias decaying factorially in M . Optimizing jointly over the number of slices M and the number of independent blocks N interpolates between the standard qDrift protocol at long times and a high-precision regime where the query cost grows slower than any power of 1 /ϵ , without requiring ancillary qubits. Numerical simulations of five molecular Hamiltonians confirm this advantage, with PRHS achieving accuracies two to four orders of magnitude beyond qDrift at equal query cost. 

Introduction .— Hamiltonian simulation is one of the central primitives of quantum computing. In its simplest form, it is the task of approximating, to within a target accuracy ϵ, the time-evolution operator U (t) = e−iHt 

generated by a Hamiltonian H. Beyond its natural relevance for simulating quantum systems [1], Hamilto-nian simulation constitutes the algorithmic backbone of numerous quantum algorithms, including phase estima-tion [2], optimization [3], and linear-system solvers [4]. operator-splitting techniques, in which the evolution gen-erated by a complicated Hamiltonian is approximated through compositions of evolutions generated by simpler Hamiltonian terms. More explicitly, given a Hamiltonian of the form

Such methods require no ancillary qubits or block encodings, and are conceptually simple and easy to implement. Within this family, the p-th order Trotter–Suzuki formula achieves a query complexity scaling as

Tuning p as a function of both ϵ and t allows product formulas to reach a quasi-optimal scaling in both quantities. In practice, however, this query complex-ity retains a polynomial dependence on Γ, a factor that becomes substantial for Hamiltonians with many non-commuting terms. This observation motivated Campbell’s proposal of the qDrift protocol [8]: in this randomized version of prod-uct formulas, the evolution time is divided into N equal time steps, but each step evolves under a single term Hγ ,randomly sampled from a classical distribution μγ . This simple procedure achieves the same asymptotic scaling in 

t and ϵ as the first-order Trotter–Suzuki formula, while eliminating the explicit dependence on Γ. qDrift, how-ever, is intrinsically limited to first order in t, so its query complexity remains linear in 1 /ϵ , just like fixed-order Trotter–Suzuki formulas. Several works have proposed extensions to higher order, each at the cost of one of qDrift’s defining features: qSWIFT [9] reaches arbitrary order by importance-sampling a signed quasi-probability distribution over higher-order terms, but its estimation procedure requires an ancilla qubit, forfeiting qDrift’s ancilla-free simplicity; composite qDrift-product formu-las [10] remain ancilla-free by instead partitioning the Hamiltonian into a stochastically sampled part and a deterministically Trotterized part, but thereby reintro-duce an explicit dependence on the number of terms Γ through the Trotterized component; and, most recently, qSHIFT [11] adaptively updates the sampling distribu-tion over blocks of r terms to reach order r, at the cost of solving a system of Γ r classical equations at every round, an overhead that grows rapidly with the target order. An arbitrary-order generalization of qDrift that simultaneously preserves its ancilla-free simplicity, its in-dependence from Γ, and an efficiently computable sam-pling rule has therefore remained an open problem. In this Letter we overcome this limitation. We show that qDrift can be systematically extended to arbitrary order M in Λ t by replacing its independent, single-term sampling rule with a suitably correlated – and, in general, signed – quasi-probability distribution over sequences of M Hamiltonian terms, a construc-arXiv:2608.29756v1 [quant-ph] 30 Aug 2026 2tion we call Pathwise Random Hamiltonian Simulation (PRHS). We prove that this distribution is unique, derive its closed-form expression, and establish a rigorous bias bound that decays factorially in M . We further show that, once the evolution time and target accuracy ϵ are fixed, the optimal choice of block size M and number of repetitions N interpolates between the standard qDrift protocol, recovered in the long-time limit, and a high-precision regime in which the query cost grows slower than any power of 1 /ϵ , all while retaining the ancilla-free simplicity of randomized product formulas. We val-idate these predictions numerically on the dynamics of five molecular Hamiltonians, where PRHS achieves ac-curacies two to four orders of magnitude beyond qDrift at equal query cost per run. We note, however, that our analysis is limited in scope, in that we only compare against other Trotter–Suzuki-type decompositions; approaches outside this family, most notably the quantum singular value transforma-tion [12, 13], achieve a better asymptotic scaling in both the evolution time and the target accuracy ϵ, at the price of ancillary qubits and a more involved implementation.

Numerical results .— Since PRHS and qDrift coincide exactly in the long-time limit Λ T → ∞ (where M ∗ = 1), a meaningful numerical comparison must instead target the regime in which correlating multiple slices, M > 1, becomes necessary to reach a fixed accuracy – the same mechanism underlying the high-accuracy limit ϵ → 0derived above. We therefore fix ϵ = 10 −1 and scan ΛT ∈ [0 , 0.9], comparing the N = 1 instance of PRHS against qDrift, allowing M to grow with Λ T as needed to reach the target accuracy. For each value of Λ T , we take the smallest M (and hence C run = M ) meeting the target accuracy, and run qDrift with a matching num-ber of steps equal to this same C run , so that PRHS and qDrift are compared at equal query cost. We evaluate this benchmark on five molecular Hamiltonians built in the STO-3G basis [15]: H2 and HeH + are retained at the full STO-3G level, while LiH, BeH 2, and H 2O are reduced to a complete active space [16] of two electrons in two spatial orbitals [CAS(2 , 2)] under the frozen-core approximation. Under the Jordan–Wigner transforma-tion [17], these representations map onto 2, 2, 4, 4, and 4 qubits, respectively. For each system, time evo-lution starts from the single-determinant Hartree-Fock (HF) reference state, the standard mean-field baseline from which real-time electron-correlation dynamics de-velop, corresponding to the computational-basis states

Conclusion .— We have introduced pathwise random hamiltonian simulation, a systematic extension of the qDrift protocol to arbitrary order M in the evolution time, obtained by replacing qDrift’s independent, single-term sampling rule with a correlated quasi-probability distribution over sequences of M Hamiltonian terms. On real hardware, however, the implementation cost of eΛtLγ /M generically varies across terms and de-pends on the target architecture. The resource-optimal importance-sampling framework of Ref. [18] is built pre-cisely for this setting: given the distribution of imple-mentation costs of eΛtLγ across γ on a specific archi-tecture, it jointly minimizes a net-cost figure of merit combining these architecture-dependent costs with the resulting estimator variance, reweighting the sampling distribution over γ away from the naive choice ∝ | qM (α)|

used here, while provably preserving the estimator’s bias. Combining this architecture-aware reweighting with the (N, M ) optimization of Eq.
